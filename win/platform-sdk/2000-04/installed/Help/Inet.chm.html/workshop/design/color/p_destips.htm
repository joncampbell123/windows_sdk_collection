Design Tips

October 21, 1997

from Pantone, Inc.
Ellen Pinto

Color adds information and increases image content. It establishes qualitative differences and highlights key data.

When selecting color for Web design, choose the background color first. The background color occupies more color space and provides contrast to the type.

Always be sure there is a high contrast between the background color and the text, title, and graphics.

Complementary colors used together, such as orange and blue, are the most interesting to the eye. To use them effectively, let one color dominate.

Since the eye sees bright colors first, emphasize key data with bright colors.

The most legible color combinations, in order, are:

1. black on yellow
2. yellow on black
3. green on white
4. red on white
5. black on white
6. white on blue
7. blue on white
8. blue on yellow

Because they are distracting and can cause a flickering effect, the least legible colors are:

1. green on orange
2. red on green
3. red on orange
4. yellow on orange
5. orange on blue
6. white on yellow

A word or phrase printed in a different color from the surrounding words in a continuing sequence will be more easily remembered by the viewer. For example, when announcing the details and specific date of a special event, the date is best printed in a color which differs from the rest of the text.

Avoid using red and green together. Many people have red/green color blindness, and cannot differentiate between the colors.

Light, medium, or deep, cool colors -- such as blue, blue-green, and violet -- have a calming effect.

Warm colors -- such as red, yellow, and orange -- excite and stimulate.

Deep tones such as black, navy, and dark gray evoke power and strength.
